What a diversion circle looks like

A diversion circle is drawn around an alternate airport. Its radius is the one-engine-inoperative speed multiplied by the diversion time. The table shows the radius for three example speeds. Your aircraft's approved speed is what counts, and it is not the same for every type.

Key terms

  • Threshold time: the range to an en-route alternate, set by the State, beyond which an EDTO approval is needed.
  • Maximum diversion time: the longest diversion time the operator's approval allows.
  • Adequate aerodrome: can handle the aeroplane. Suitable aerodrome: adequate, with weather and runway conditions good enough at the time of use.
  • Equal time point (ETP): where continuing and returning take the same time.
  • EDTO-significant system: a system whose time limit can restrict how far the flight may go, for example cargo fire suppression.

FAQ

Is EDTO only for twin-engine aeroplanes?

It applies to turbine-engine aeroplanes with two or more engines, though the thresholds differ. The older term ETOPS covered twins.

Can I use these tools for real planning?

No. They are training tools. Use your approved data, your operator's procedures and your State's rules.
