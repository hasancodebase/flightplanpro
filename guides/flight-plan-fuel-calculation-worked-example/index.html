Flight Plan Fuel Calculation: A Worked Example with Block Fuel

Fuel planning builds block fuel from several parts: taxi, trip, contingency, alternate and final reserve. This example works through a whole flight in kilograms, so you can see how the parts add up. The numbers are invented for practice.

The flight

  • Distance to destination: 1,200 NM at a ground speed of 440 knots.
  • Alternate: 150 NM from the destination at 400 knots.
  • Cruise fuel burn: 2,400 kg per hour. Holding fuel burn: 2,100 kg per hour.
  • Taxi fuel: 200 kg.

Step by step

PartWorkingFuel (kg)
Trip fuel1,200 NM / 440 kt = 2.73 h, then 2.73 h x 2,4006,545
Contingency5 percent of trip fuel327
Alternate fuel150 NM / 400 kt = 0.375 h, then 0.375 h x 2,400900
Final reserve30 minutes at 2,100 kg per hour1,050
TaxiGiven200
Block fuelAdd the five parts9,022

Why each part is there

  • Trip fuel takes the aeroplane from takeoff to landing at the destination.
  • Contingency fuel covers unexpected extra burn. A common figure under ICAO standards is 5 percent of the trip fuel, though other methods are allowed.
  • Alternate fuel flies the aeroplane to the alternate if a landing at the destination is not possible.
  • Final reserve fuel is 30 minutes for turbine-engine aeroplanes and 45 minutes for piston-engine aeroplanes, at holding speed 1,500 ft above the aerodrome, under ICAO standards.

Check the result

Endurance on 9,022 kg at the cruise burn of 2,400 kg per hour is about 3 hours 45 minutes, which is more than the 2 hours 44 minutes trip plus the diversion and reserve. Then check that the takeoff weight with this fuel is within the aircraft's limits, using a load sheet.

This is a simplified training example. It leaves out climb and descent differences, wind and any additional fuel. Your regulator and operator set the real rules, so follow your fuel policy.

FAQ

How do you calculate block fuel?

Add taxi fuel, trip fuel, contingency fuel, alternate fuel and final reserve fuel, plus any additional fuel the commander decides to carry.

How much is contingency fuel?

A common figure under ICAO standards is 5 percent of the trip fuel, though other methods are allowed. Follow your regulator's rules.
