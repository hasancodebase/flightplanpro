What Is in an ICAO Flight Plan? Every Item Explained

The ICAO flight plan is the standard form pilots and operators use to tell air traffic services about a flight. It is filled in as numbered items, and the message version starts with (FPL.

The items

  • Item 7: aircraft identification, the callsign or registration.
  • Item 8: flight rules (I, V, Y or Z) and type of flight (S scheduled, N non-scheduled, G general aviation, M military, X other).
  • Item 9: aircraft type designator and wake turbulence category (L, M, H or J).
  • Item 10: radio, navigation and surveillance equipment.
  • Item 13: departure aerodrome and estimated off-block time (EOBT) in UTC.
  • Item 15: cruising speed, cruising level and route.
  • Item 16: destination aerodrome, total estimated elapsed time, and alternate aerodromes.
  • Item 18: other information, such as PBN/ capabilities, DOF/ date of flight and REG/ registration.
  • Item 19: supplementary information, such as endurance, persons on board and emergency equipment.

Worked example

(FPL-ABC123-IN
-A320/M-SDE3FGHIRWY/LB1
-OPKC0800
-N0450F350 DCT 25N063E DCT 25N059E
-OMDB0137 OMSJ
-PBN/A1B1C1D1L1O1S2 DOF/261016)

ABC123 flies IFR (I) as a non-scheduled flight (N). The aircraft is an A320 of medium wake category. It leaves Karachi (OPKC) at 08:00 UTC at 450 knots (N0450) at flight level 350 (F350), going direct through two latitude and longitude points. The elapsed time to Dubai (OMDB) is 1 hour 37 minutes, with Sharjah (OMSJ) as the alternate. DOF/261016 is the date of flight, 16 October 2026.

Filing

FAQ

What does DCT mean in a route?

Direct. It tells air traffic control that the aircraft will fly straight from one point to the next, not along an airway.

What is EOBT?

Estimated off-block time: when the aircraft is expected to start moving for departure, in UTC.
