How to Calculate Crosswind and Headwind Components

Before every takeoff and landing you compare the wind with the runway. The wind splits into a headwind (or tailwind) along the runway and a crosswind across it. Knowing both tells you whether the landing is within your limits.

The formula

First find the angle between the wind direction and the runway heading. Then:

  • Crosswind = wind speed × sin(angle)
  • Headwind = wind speed × cos(angle). A negative result means a tailwind.

Worked example

Runway 24 has a heading of 240°. The wind is 270° at 20 knots, so the angle is 30°. Crosswind = 20 × 0.5 = 10 knots. Headwind = 20 × 0.87 = about 17 knots.

Quick mental method

  • 30° off the runway: crosswind is about half the wind speed, headwind about 85 percent.
  • 45° off: both are about 70 percent.
  • 60° off: crosswind is about 85 to 90 percent, headwind about half.
  • 90° off: all of the wind is crosswind and there is no headwind.

Things that catch people out

  • True or magnetic. Runway headings are magnetic. Wind in a METAR is given in degrees true, so correct for magnetic variation first. Wind from ATIS or the tower is already magnetic.
  • Gusts. Use the gust speed for a cautious figure.
  • Limits. Your aircraft manual and your operator set the crosswind limit. Wet or contaminated runways usually lower it.

FAQ

Is the crosswind component the same as the wind speed?

Only when the wind is exactly 90 degrees to the runway. At any other angle the crosswind is smaller than the wind speed.

How do I know if it is a tailwind?

If the angle between the wind and the runway heading is more than 90 degrees, the cosine is negative and the headwind component becomes a tailwind.
