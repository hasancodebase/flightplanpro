How to Read a TAF: Airport Forecasts Explained

A TAF is an airport forecast. It reads like a METAR, with added time groups and change groups that tell you when conditions are expected to move.

The header

TAF OMDB 161100Z 1612/1718 means a forecast for Dubai issued on day 16 at 11:00 UTC, valid from day 16 at 12:00 until day 17 at 18:00. Most TAFs cover 24 or 30 hours.

The change groups

  • FM (from): conditions change at the stated time. FM161800 means from 18:00 on day 16. Everything after it replaces the earlier forecast.
  • BECMG (becoming): a gradual change during the stated period, for example BECMG 1700/1702.
  • TEMPO: temporary fluctuations lasting less than an hour at a time, within the stated period.
  • PROB30 / PROB40: a 30 or 40 percent chance of the stated conditions.

Worked example

TAF OMDB 161100Z 1612/1718 31008KT 6000 HZ FEW030 TEMPO 1620/1624 4000 BECMG 1700/1702 36010KT

Base forecast: wind 310° at 8 knots, visibility 6,000 m in haze, few clouds at 3,000 ft. Between 20:00 and 24:00 on day 16, visibility may temporarily drop to 4,000 m. Between 00:00 and 02:00 on day 17 the wind becomes 360° at 10 knots.

Using a TAF in planning

  • Look at the forecast for your estimated arrival time, plus and minus one hour.
  • Take the worst applicable group, including TEMPO and PROB, when deciding on fuel and alternates.
  • Check the issue time. A TAF can be amended, and the latest one wins.

FAQ

What does NSW mean in a TAF?

No significant weather. It ends a weather condition that was forecast earlier in the TAF.

How is a TAF different from a METAR?

A METAR reports what the weather is now. A TAF forecasts what it will be over the next day or so.
