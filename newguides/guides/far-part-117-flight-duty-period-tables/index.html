FAR Part 117 Limits: Flight Time and Flight Duty Period Tables Explained

FAR Part 117 limits how long US airline pilots can fly and work. Three tables do most of the work. Table A caps flight time for two-pilot crews, Table B caps the flight duty period (FDP) for two-pilot crews, and Table C caps the FDP for augmented crews. The numbers below are from 14 CFR Part 117 as published in the eCFR, checked in October 2026.

Table A: flight time for two-pilot crews

The limit depends on the time the flight duty period starts, in the time zone where the crew is acclimated.

The longest FDP in the table is 19 hours: four pilots, a Class 1 rest facility, and a start between 0700 and 1259.

Other rules that go with the tables

  • Flight time with augmented crews: 13 hours with three pilots and 17 hours with four pilots (section 117.11).
  • Segments: augmented operations are limited to three flight segments (section 117.17).
  • In-flight rest: two consecutive hours in the second half of the FDP for the pilot flying the landing, and 90 minutes for the monitoring pilot.
  • Not acclimated: a 30 minute reduction applies to the FDP limits.
  • Rest before an FDP: at least 10 consecutive hours, with an 8 hour uninterrupted sleep opportunity, and 30 consecutive hours free from duty in any 168 hours (section 117.25).

What these tables leave out

Extensions of the FDP, split duty, reserve duty and the cumulative weekly, 28-day and yearly limits are separate parts of the rule. Check the regulation and your operator's approved program for real planning.

FAQ

What is the maximum flight duty period under Part 117?

19 hours. It applies to a four-pilot crew with a Class 1 rest facility whose FDP starts between 0700 and 1259.

Does Table B apply to augmented crews?

No. Table B is for two-pilot crews. Augmented crews use Table C, which depends on the number of pilots and the rest facility class.
