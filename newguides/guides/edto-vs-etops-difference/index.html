EDTO vs ETOPS: What Is the Difference?

ETOPS and EDTO describe the same idea: flying an aeroplane far from airports where it could land after an engine failure or another serious problem. ETOPS is the older term, used for twin-engine aeroplanes, and the FAA still uses it. EDTO is the term ICAO adopted, and it covers all turbine-engine aeroplanes with two or more engines.

What stays the same

  • Operating beyond the threshold needs an approval from the State.
  • Approvals come in levels. SKYbrary describes up to 90 minutes, beyond 90 and up to 180 minutes, and beyond 180 minutes, with 15 percent increases for specific routes.
  • The operator must name adequate en-route alternates for the route, plan the critical fuel scenario and equal time points, and check weather for the alternates.

Why both words are still used

ICAO standards describe the operation as EDTO, while the FAA's rules and guidance use ETOPS. Airlines and manuals often use whichever term their authority uses, so you will see both.

FAQ

Is EDTO replacing ETOPS?

EDTO is the term in ICAO Annex 6, and the FAA continues to use ETOPS. They describe the same kind of operation, so operators often use both.

Does ETOPS apply to four-engine aeroplanes?

FAA guidance applies beyond 180 minutes to passenger aeroplanes with more than two engines. ICAO's EDTO applies to turbine aeroplanes with two or more engines.
